If you run a sales or support team, you have probably wished you could hear what is really said on calls. Recordings help you coach agents, settle disputes and understand why customers say yes or no. Here is how teams do it, and what to check first.

## Why record calls

- **Coaching.** Play real calls to show what works.
- **Disputes.** Check what was actually promised.
- **Follow-up.** Make sure nothing agreed gets forgotten.
- **Understanding customers.** Hear the questions and objections that come up again and again.

## The problem many teams have

Many teams start with an online calling system. Then customers stop answering, because networks often flag unfamiliar internet calls as spam. So agents quietly switch to their personal mobile phones, and the calls that really matter happen where nobody can see or record them.

Forcing agents back onto a system that does not work is not the answer. The better approach is to capture calls where they already happen.

## Ways to record calls

1. **An online dialer with built-in recording.** Works well if customers actually answer those calls.
2. **Manual recording.** Agents record themselves. Easy to forget and hard to check.
3. **A recording app on the agent's phone.** It detects calls, records them, and sends the audio and details to your customer system automatically.

## Check the law and get consent

**Rules about recording calls differ between countries and even between regions, and they change.** In many places you need the consent of the people on the call. This is general information, not legal advice, so check the rules where you and your customers are, and ask a lawyer if you are unsure.

Good habits regardless of where you are:

- **Tell people.** Say at the start of the call that it may be recorded.
- **Record for a clear purpose,** such as quality and training.
- **Keep recordings secure,** with limited access.
- **Do not keep them forever.** Decide how long you need them.
- **Have a written policy** that your team understands.

## Choosing a tool: questions to ask

1. Does it work with agents' own phones?
2. Where are recordings stored, and who owns them?
3. Does it connect to our CRM?
4. What happens when the phone has no internet?
5. Can we control who hears the recordings?
