Many owners ask for "an app" when what they need is a website, and the other way round. Choosing the wrong one costs time and money. Here is the difference in plain language.

## A website

A website is a set of pages that tell people who you are and what you offer. Visitors read it, contact you, and sometimes buy. Google can find it, which is why a website is the base of almost every business's online presence.

**You probably need a website if** you want customers to find you on Google, show your services, and make it easy to get in touch.

## A web app

A web app looks like a website, but people do things in it: sign in, fill forms, see their own information, manage bookings or view reports. It runs in the browser, so there is nothing to install.

**You probably need a web app if** customers or staff need their own accounts, dashboards or a system to manage orders, bookings or records.

## A mobile app

A mobile app is installed on a phone from an app store. It can use phone features such as the camera, notifications or, on Android, calls and location. It can also work with poor internet if built for that.

**You probably need a mobile app if** you need a phone feature that a browser cannot use well, or your customers will use the product every day and expect it on their home screen.

## How to choose

| If you want to… | Start with |
|---|---|
| Be found on Google and show what you do | A website |
| Let people sign in and manage things | A web app |
| Use phone features or work all day on a phone | A mobile app |
| Try an idea cheaply | A simple web app or website first |

## Common questions

**Can I have all three?** Yes. Many businesses start with a website, add a web app, and build a mobile app only when there is a clear reason.

**Do I need an app to look professional?** No. A fast, clear website often does more for a small business than an app nobody downloads.

**What decides the cost?** Mainly how many features it has, how much has to be designed from scratch, and how many other systems it must connect to. A short discovery conversation lets a good developer give you a fixed-scope estimate.

## A good first step

Write down what you want a visitor to do: call you, book, buy, sign in. The answer usually points to the right choice.
